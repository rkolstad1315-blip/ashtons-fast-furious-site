A dog's routine is the first thing to go when orders come in, and it is also the thing that keeps the dog steady while everything else changes. If you are a Camp Lejeune or MCAS New River family facing a PCS, field time, or a deployment, this post is about holding the dog's day together: exercise, meals, and sleep, through the move and the months after.

## Why routine changes stress dogs

Dogs do not understand orders. They understand that the boxes came out, one person is gone, the walk moved, and nobody is sure where the food bowl lives now. The stress shows up at home as:

- Pacing, whining, or following you from room to room
- Accidents in a house trained dog
- Chewing or digging that had stopped years ago
- Going off food, or guarding it
- Barking at things that never used to matter

None of that is spite. It is a dog asking for the parts of the day that are predictable. The more of the old routine you can hold, especially exercise and meal times, the faster the rest settles.

## Before the move: records, microchip, travel

[Military OneSource](https://www.militaryonesource.mil/moving-housing/moving-with-pets/) sums up the first step as "know before you go": learn the new installation's and community's pet rules well ahead of time, including licensing, breed limits, and required health documents. A few things to handle early:

- **Vet records.** Get a current copy of vaccination records and any prescriptions. If you are flying, ask about health certificate timing.
- **Microchip.** Military OneSource suggests asking your vet about an identification microchip if your dog does not already have one, and updating the contact details on the one your dog has.
- **Travel prep.** Ask your vet about motion sickness and travel stress if your dog is not used to the car or a flight. Practice short crate sessions and car rides before the long one.
- **Movers do not take animals.** Your moving company cannot ship live animals, so the dog travels with you or through a pet shipper you arrange.
- **Housing rules.** Some off base neighborhoods have HOAs that limit breed, number, or size of pets, and not every hotel on the route takes dogs. Call ahead.

Reimbursement rules for moving a pet change, so check the current Joint Travel Regulations or ask Military OneSource rather than relying on a figure you read last year.

## During a deployment: keeping exercise steady when one parent is solo

When one adult is gone, the dog's exercise is usually the first thing squeezed out of the day. The parent at home is covering school runs, work, and bedtime alone, and a long evening walk is not happening.

A few ways to keep it steady:

- **Shorten and schedule.** Two short walks at fixed times beat one long walk that never happens. Dogs care more about the pattern than the mileage.
- **Trade off with a neighbor or another military spouse.** Many families near Camp Lejeune are in the same spot; a walk swap helps both dogs.
- **Mental work on the hard days.** A puzzle feeder, a scent game, or ten minutes of training takes the edge off when a walk is impossible.
- **Bring help to the driveway.** A supervised treadmill session at home means no drop off, no extra errand, and no new people or dogs for an already stressed dog to cope with. Sessions can be scheduled around deployment and PCS dates.

## Settling into new housing, on base or off

The first weeks in a new house are when the old routine pays off. Keep the same meal times and the same bedtime. Walk the same route for the first week or two so the dog learns one patch of the new neighborhood well before exploring. Set up the crate or bed in a quiet spot on day one, with something that smells like the old house.

Expect a regression. A dog that was reliable may bark more, pull more, or have an accident. Go back to basics for a couple of weeks: shorter walks, more reward for calm behavior, and plenty of sleep. If anxiety is severe or not improving, check with your vet first; some dogs need medical help alongside the routine.

## Help at home that does not mean boarding

Boarding and daycare mean a drop off, a new building, and a crowd of unfamiliar dogs, which is a lot for a dog whose world just changed. Options that keep the dog home:

- A trusted dog walker or sitter who comes to you
- A neighbor swap for midday breaks
- A supervised treadmill session in your driveway, so the dog gets a real workout without leaving home

With us, the climate controlled unit parks in your driveway, a person supervises every minute, and your dog wears a harness and is never tied by the neck. Sessions run 15 to 60 minutes and supplement walks rather than replace them.

## A short checklist

1. Pull vet records and update the microchip contact before the move.
2. Pick two fixed walk times that survive a solo week, and protect them.
3. Line up one source of exercise that does not depend on you having a free hour.
