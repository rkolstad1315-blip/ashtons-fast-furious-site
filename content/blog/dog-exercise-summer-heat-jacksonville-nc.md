From June through September, the safest time to exercise a dog in Jacksonville, NC is early morning or after sunset, and on the worst days the safest place is indoors. Coastal humidity makes a hot afternoon harder on a dog than the thermometer suggests, and the pavement can burn paws long before the air feels dangerous to you.

This post covers why humidity matters, the warning signs of overheating, how to check the pavement, when to walk, and what to do with a stir crazy dog when you cannot walk at all.

## Why humidity makes heat worse for dogs

Dogs cool themselves mainly by panting, which works by evaporating moisture from the tongue and airways. Humid air slows that evaporation, so a dog loses the ability to shed heat on exactly the days it needs to most. That is why a muggy afternoon in Onslow County can be more dangerous than a drier day at the same temperature.

The [American Veterinary Medical Association](https://www.avma.org/resources/pet-owners/petcare/warm-weather-pet-safety) warns that warm weather is a health risk even on days that do not seem that hot to you. Flat faced breeds, overweight dogs, seniors, and dogs with heart or breathing problems are at higher risk, and a thick double coat does not help a Husky or a Shepherd in August.

## Warning signs of overheating

Know these before you need them. The AVMA lists the signs of heat stress as anxiety, excessive panting, restlessness, excessive drooling, unsteadiness, abnormal gum and tongue color, and collapse, and it advises emergency veterinary care if you see any of them.

If your dog shows these signs, stop the activity, move to shade or air conditioning, offer water, wet the body with cool (not ice cold) water, and call your vet or an emergency clinic. Do not wait to see if it passes. Check with your vet first about anything specific to your dog's health.

## The pavement test

Asphalt and concrete soak up heat all day and stay hot after the sun drops. Before a walk, press the back of your hand flat on the pavement where your dog will walk. If it is uncomfortable for you to hold it there, it is too hot for paw pads. Walk on grass, wait for the surface to cool, or skip the walk.

The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/about-us/press-releases/aspca-shares-lifesaving-tips-keep-pets-safe-above-average-temperatures-are) also reminds owners that pets dehydrate quickly in heat, so carry water on every summer walk and keep fresh water and a shady spot available at home.

## The best walking windows

In summer the pattern in Jacksonville, NC is simple:

- **Early morning**, before the sun is high, is the coolest the day will get.
- **After sunset** is the second window, once the pavement passes the hand test.
- **Midday and late afternoon** are for bathroom breaks on grass, in the shade, and back inside.

Keep the midday walk short and purposeful. Save the longer outing for the cool windows, and if the day is a scorcher with no cool window at all, the walk can wait. One skipped walk is not a problem. A heat emergency is.

## Indoor and climate controlled options

The hard part is not skipping the walk. It is living with a dog that did not get exercise. A few options that work indoors:

- **Scent games.** Hide treats around the house and let the dog hunt. Nose work tires a dog faster than you would think.
- **Training sessions.** Ten minutes of new behaviors is real work for the brain.
- **Tug and fetch down a hallway**, in short rounds, with water breaks.
- **A supervised treadmill session** inside a climate controlled unit, parked in your driveway.

## A summer checklist

1. Walk early or after dark, and hand test the pavement first.
2. Carry water, and keep the midday outing to a bathroom break on grass.
3. Learn the warning signs, and call the vet at the first one.
4. Plan an indoor outlet for the days you cannot walk at all.
