Your dog's first treadmill session is a short, supervised introduction, not a workout, and it is free. The point of the first visit is to find out whether your dog is comfortable on the treadmill and how long they should run next time. Nobody is forced to keep going, and most of the visit is spent going slowly.

This post walks through the whole first visit so there are no surprises: what to tell us beforehand, what happens when the unit pulls up, how the intro works, what you do during the session, and what happens after.

## Before the visit

When you [book the free session](/book/), the form asks for your town, how many dogs, dog size, preferred days and time of day, and your contact details. Breed and notes are optional but useful; tell us if your dog is nervous around strangers, has a health condition, or has never worn a harness.

Two things to sort out before we arrive:

- **Vaccinations.** The required list is [CONFIRM]. For reference, veterinary guidelines from the [American Animal Hospital Association](https://www.aaha.org/canine-vaccinations) describe a set of core vaccines recommended for all dogs; your vet will know what your dog has had.
- **Vet clearance.** Senior dogs run only after a vet has cleared the workout, and we recommend the same for any dog with a joint, heart, or breathing condition. Puppy and senior rules are [CONFIRM]. When in doubt, check with your vet first.

## When the unit arrives

The climate controlled unit parks in your driveway. It holds two dog treadmills, so two dogs can run side by side. The driveway space we need is [CONFIRM]. Your dog stays home the whole time, with no drop off and no other people's dogs.

Inside, the temperature stays controlled, which matters most in a Jacksonville, NC summer. The workout happens out of the heat even when the driveway is baking.

## The intro: harness, slow start, never tied by the neck

The first visit starts with a short intro so your dog can try the treadmill before a longer run. Your dog wears a harness for the session. We never tie a dog by the neck. Harness provided is [CONFIRM].

The intro steps we follow with a new dog are [CONFIRM]. Throughout, the rule is the same: your dog is never forced to keep running. If your dog wants off, the session pauses or ends, and that is useful information, not a failure. Some dogs take to it in minutes. Some need a second visit before they relax. Both are normal.

A person supervises every minute, from the moment the treadmill starts until your dog is done. You can read how we handle supervision, the harness, water breaks, heat, and cleaning on the [safety page](/safety/).

## What your dog does, and what you do

During the session your dog walks or runs on the treadmill while the handler watches posture, breathing, and body language and adjusts as needed. Water and rest breaks are part of every session.

You are welcome to be there. For a nervous dog, having you in sight can help. For a dog that keeps looking back at you instead of settling into the work, stepping out of view for a few minutes often helps more. We will tell you what we are seeing.

## After the session

Your dog gets water and a cool down, and the treadmills and the unit are cleaned between dogs. Then we talk about what we saw and pick the next session length. Sessions run 15 to 60 minutes; a 30 minute session is $60, and the first session is free. Full prices are on the [pricing page](/pricing/).

Expect a tired, settled dog that evening. If anything about the visit raised a health question, check with your vet first before the next one.

## A quick summary

1. Book the free session and tell us about your dog.
2. Have vaccination records handy, and a vet's okay if your dog is a senior or has a condition.
3. Expect a slow intro, a harness, a person at your dog's side the whole time, and a plan for next time.
